Similarity Search vs Semantic Search are closely related, but they are not exactly the same.

The simplest difference is:
    - Similarity Search: Finds things that are similar based on a comparison method.
    - Semantic Search: Finds things that have similar meanings, even when the words are different.

Similarity search compares your query with the stored sentences using a similarity measure.

Semantic search focuses on understanding the meaning behind your query, rather than just matching words.

Here's what happens:
1. The user asks: "How do I repair my computer?"
2. An embedding model converts the question into a numerical vector.
3. The vector database stores embeddings of your documents.
4. Similarity search compares the query vector against stored vectors using cosine similarity or another distance metric.
5. The system retrieves documents that are semantically relevant.
Important: Semantic search often uses similarity search internally.

So they are not two completely separate techniques.
